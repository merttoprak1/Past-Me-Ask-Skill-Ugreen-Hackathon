# Use cases

What Past Me Asks is for, linked from [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md). The people below are made up, and none of this comes from a real run.

## What we are solving

People talk to their phones and write notes for years. Hidden in them are sentences aimed at the future: predictions, promises, worries and questions to the person they would become. Those sentences were written to be read later, yet nobody goes back to hear them. Archive search does not help, because you cannot search for a promise you no longer remember making.

## Aims

1. **Let the past ask:** find the sentences past you addressed to the future, quoted exactly and dated.
2. **Let today answer:** record the answer in your own words, typed or as a new memo, and keep both sides as one dated dialogue.
3. **No verdicts:** only you decide whether a promise was kept or a prediction came true.
4. **Private by design, honest about limits:** the Skill reads only the folders you name and itself sends nothing out; where UGOS runs its AI is not documented, so it tells you before reading.

## What it does not do

- It does not give advice, diagnose or tell you what your words mean.
- It does not score your past self or count how many predictions came true.
- It does not put words in anyone's mouth: questions and summaries are marked as written by the Skill.
- It does not search video files, because transcription of video is not documented.
- It never reuses its own saved dialogues or answers as "past me".

## Situations

### 1. A birthday check-in (Past Me Asks)
**Ana** turns 35. She asks what past her expected from her thirties. The Skill finds a 2019 memo, "By 35 I want to have run a marathon", and a 2021 note promising to learn Portuguese. She answers both by voice memo and marks the marathon "kept".

### 2. A promise kept quietly (Past Me Asks)
**Jonas** asks what he promised himself about his family. A 2022 note says "I will call Dad every Sunday." He types what happened and leaves it unmarked. The dialogue file is the first thing he has written about it.

### 3. A worry that did not come true (Past Me Asks)
**Sofia** asks what past her was afraid of about her teaching job. Three dated worries come back. She answers each one today; the Skill makes no comment on whether the worries were justified.

### 4. A creator's then-and-now video (both modes)
**Theo** runs a channel. Past Me Asks finds the predictions he made in his first year; he answers them on camera with Now Me memos, and the saved dialogue becomes the script.

### 5. A big decision (Ask Past Me)
**Priya** is deciding whether to move abroad again. She asks what she thought about her last move and gets a dated timeline of her own words from before, during and after it.

### 6. Building the library overnight (Build library)
**Ben** has 600 memos. He runs Build library in batches of 40, so later questions are answered from the saved, tagged transcripts without transcribing again.

## Who benefits most

People with years of voice memos, notes or journals on the NAS: anyone at a milestone, creators, founders and writers. Someone with only a few recordings gets a short list, and the Skill says how much it searched.
